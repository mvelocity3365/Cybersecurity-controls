# Cybersecurity-controls
<b><h1>1. Define cybersecurity controls.</b></h1>

<p>Organizations use cybersecurity controls—policies, processes, technologies, and practices—to safeguard their data, networks, and information systems from online attacks. By stopping unwanted access, identifying malicious activity, and efficiently handling security incidents, these policies are intended to lower security risks. By protecting sensitive data from cyberattacks, unintentional loss, and system failures, cybersecurity measures contribute to the preservation of information's confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Organizations can reduce vulnerabilities, adhere to legal and regulatory obligations, and preserve stakeholder and consumer trust by putting in place the proper security controls.</p>

<b><h1>2. Explain the three categories of security controls</b></h1>

<p>Administrative, technological, and physical controls are the three primary categories into which cybersecurity controls are typically divided. Administrative controls are management-driven methods that create security rules, processes, and guidelines to make sure staff members are aware of their duties for safeguarding company data. Through governance, training, and compliance, these controls aim to lower risks associated with people. Technical controls are technology-based defenses against cyber risks and unauthorized access to computer systems, networks, and data. To monitor, identify, and stop assaults, they rely on hardware and software solutions. Physical controls are safeguards against environmental risks, theft, vandalism, and unauthorized access to an organization's physical infrastructure, which includes buildings, equipment, and devices. When combined, these three areas offer a thorough approach to information security.</p>

<b><h1>3. Give three examples of each category</h1></b>

<p>Information security rules and procedures, employee background checks, and security awareness and training programs are examples of administrative controls. These steps aid in ensuring that employees are aware of security regulations and adhere to them. Firewalls, antivirus programs, and multi-factor authentication (MFA) are examples of technical controls. By filtering harmful traffic, identifying malware, and confirming user identities before allowing access, these technologies aid in system protection. Security personnel, surveillance cameras (CCTV), and biometric access control systems are examples of physical controls. These precautions aid in keeping sensitive data, facilities, and equipment out of the hands of unauthorized people.</p>

<b><h1>4. Explain preventive, detective, and corrective controls with examples</h1></b>

<p>Security techniques known as preventive controls are intended to avert cyber disasters before they happen. Reducing vulnerabilities and preventing unwanted access to systems and data is their main goal. Strong password restrictions that lower the possibility of unauthorized access, firewalls that stop hostile network traffic, and encryption that prevents unauthorized users from reading sensitive data are a few examples of preventive controls.</p>

<p>To find and track security issues that have already happened or are happening right now, detective controls are put in place. Organizations can swiftly identify suspicious activity and take action before serious harm is done thanks to these controls. Intrusion detection systems (IDS), security information and event management (SIEM) systems that examine security logs, and ongoing network monitoring that spots anomalous or illegal activity are a few examples.</p>

<p>After an attack or system failure, corrective controls assist businesses in recovering from security incidents and resuming regular operations. Their goal is to reduce damage and stop such occurrences from happening again. Examples include deploying software patches to address security flaws, recovering data from safe backups following a ransomware attack, and putting in place an incident response plan to successfully control and recover from cybersecurity incidents.</p>

<b><h1>5. List five cybersecurity best practices everyone should follow</h1></b>

<p>To lower the risk of cyberattacks and safeguard organizational and personal data, everyone should adhere to fundamental cybersecurity best practices.</p> <ol><li></li>First, users should set strong, one-of-a-kind passwords for each account and, if feasible, turn on multi-factor authentication.</li> <li></li>Second, in order to address known security flaws, software, operating systems, and applications should be updated on a regular basis.</li> <li></li>Third, since phishing assaults are among the most frequent techniques employed by cybercriminals, people should exercise caution when reading emails, clicking links, or downloading attachments.</li> <li></li>Fourth, to guarantee that it can be restored in the event that it is lost or stolen, crucial data should be routinely backed up and stored safely.</li> <li></li>Lastly, in order to identify and eliminate malware before it may harm systems or steal confidential data, users should install reliable antivirus software and keep it updated.</li></ol> <p>By adhering to these best practices, cybersecurity is greatly enhanced and the probability of successful cyberattacks is decreased.</p>
